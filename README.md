PERFORMANCE FRAMEWORK

The performance rating evaluates BOTH:

1. WHAT I achieved
   - Results
   - Objectives
   - Business/customer impact
   - OKR contribution
   - Specific measurable outcomes
   - Before/after improvements
   - Follow-through against stated objectives

2. HOW I achieved it
   - Technical/professional competencies
   - Company values
   - Communication
   - Risk management
   - Ownership
   - Collaboration
   - Judgment
   - Leadership behaviors
   - Mentoring and developing others

Evidence should be specific and observable rather than based on general impressions.

For WHAT, prioritize:
- Specific targets achieved or exceeded
- Clear attribution to team/department/company OKRs
- Quantifiable business, customer, reliability, productivity, or engineering outcomes
- Before/after comparisons
- Concrete deliverables and sustained improvements

For HOW, prioritize:
- Clear communication
- Early identification and management of risks
- Ownership and accountability
- Constructive challenge and willingness to speak up
- Bringing the right stakeholders into decisions early
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Sharing credit
- Sound judgment under ambiguity
- Knowing when to escalate and when to operate independently
- Mentoring and developing others
- Specific examples of leadership behavior

ROLE CONTEXT

Evaluate my performance specifically at the Senior / Principal Architect level.

For a Senior Architect, emphasize:
- Ownership of significant technical domains
- Architecture quality and long-term technical strategy
- Delivery of complex initiatives
- Cross-team technical influence
- Risk management
- Technical decision-making
- Mentoring
- Collaboration with engineering/product/business stakeholders

For a Principal Architect, additionally emphasize:
- Organization-wide or multi-team impact
- Setting technical direction and architecture strategy
- Solving ambiguous/high-complexity problems
- Influence without direct authority
- Establishing reusable architectural patterns and standards
- Reducing systemic technical risk
- Enabling multiple teams to move faster
- Connecting architecture decisions to business/customer outcomes
- Raising the technical capability of the organization
- Executive/stakeholder communication
- Long-term thinking and strategic trade-offs

WRITING STYLE

Write like a strong but credible Senior/Principal Architect.

The tone should be:
- confident but not boastful
- factual
- evidence-driven
- concise
- executive-friendly
- technically credible
- focused on outcomes rather than activity

Avoid:
- generic statements such as "I collaborated effectively"
- long lists of tasks
- excessive technical jargon
- claiming team accomplishments as individual accomplishments
- unsupported claims
- exaggerated adjectives such as "transformational" unless the evidence supports them
- describing architecture work without explaining why it mattered

Prefer:
- "I led..."
- "I drove..."
- "I established..."
- "I influenced..."
- "I identified..."
- "I made the architectural trade-off to..."
- "This resulted in..."
- "This reduced..."
- "This enabled..."
- "This was subsequently adopted by..."
- "The before/after outcome was..."

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Separate:
A) WHAT I personally delivered
B) WHAT I enabled others/teams to deliver
C) WHAT the broader team accomplished

Make my individual contribution explicit without taking credit for other people's work.

WHEN INFORMATION IS INCOMPLETE

"EVIDENCE GAPS / QUESTIONS"

List the most important missing information that would make the review stronger.

Prioritize questions such as:
- What measurable business/customer outcome resulted?

- What risk or cost was avoided?
- Was the architecture reused elsewhere?

- What stakeholder/peer feedback supports the claim?
- What OKR did this contribute to?
